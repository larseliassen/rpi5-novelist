# Chapter 38

The director’s office was a whirlwind of muted grey and salt-laced air, the smell clinging to everything like a living thing.  He paced before Elin, his eyes reflecting the storm brewing both outside and inside the walls of the building – a mirror for the chaos in her own gut. “Something’s changed,” he said finally, voice tight, "Anders Larsen, the alibi is crumbling.”

Mikael had come to meet Elin at the director's office earlier that morning, his face a pale mask behind tired eyes. He handed her something from his pocket – a photo of Anders standing on the dock of Fjerdesund, years ago, looking back as if into some unseen abyss. The moment seemed frozen, caught in time: the cold Norwegian air biting at their faces,  the salt spray clinging to their hair. 

"He's always claimed he had no connection with the incident," Mikael explained later that night. "But this..." He showed her a photograph of Kåre’s dead man’s chest; his eyes were wide in distress and fear. "Someone is trying to cover for him."  A wave of nausea washed over Elin, she looked away.

"What's Anders' connection?” The director asked. 

“He says he saw visions,” Mikael said, voice a low hum barely audible above the storm outside. “Memories he couldn’t explain.” He ran a hand through his thinning hair, "He kept saying about a woman and the lighthouse." 

She had spent days sifting through the case; finding bits of evidence like shattered glass in a dark alleyway – always leading to dead ends.  "Kåre's death," the director said, breaking the silence with a sharp intake of breath. "It all adds up..." 
 
The last time she’d seen him was just yesterday at Fjerdesund. The old captain had left her a message on the dock – a cryptic note hidden beneath his worn boots that he couldn't manage to write down. It was an odd request, asking for her presence in his former cabin, but as if there were something deeper going on than simple instructions or a plea for help. She hadn’t dared question it yet. 

Later, at the director’s house, Mikael had mentioned that he wanted to talk about something urgent, and brought out the photo of Anders from his case file— an old photo taken during the investigation into Kåre's death.  “He said there was a witness who saw Anders near the docks,” he whispered; “an old fisherman.” 
 
The image burned in her mind - a weathered face with deep-set eyes, a man holding a fishing rod as though trying to escape something – but what? Was it fear or hope that was reflected in his eyes?

“He told me about a woman,” she whispered back,  her voice barely audible over the howling wind. "A white figure he said haunted him." A sudden urge crept upon her - she needed to go back to Fjerdesund; gather more information. 
 
She felt a cold rush of energy course through her. The note clutched in her hand was old, as if it had belonged to the ship’s former captain—a man named Olaf. He spoke about this woman, his words painting images in her mind – a ghostly presence that seemed both inviting and terrifying,  yet the details were vague, like fragments of an unfinished dream. 

As she sat alone on the dock, watching the faint light from the lighthouse flicker against the stormy night sky, a sense of urgency filled her chest. She had to get answers, no matter what cost. Something was happening— something big. And if Anders Larsen's alibi was crumbling, then she knew that this case would take an unforeseen turn; and it felt dangerous, like stepping onto an icy precipice. The wind whipped through the air, and the lighthouse beacon seemed to blink – a silent call echoing in the darkness.  Suddenly, she noticed something—a flicker of movement on the water just beyond the horizon – was there anyone there?
