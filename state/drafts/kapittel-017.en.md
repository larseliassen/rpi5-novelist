# Chapter 17

Mikael’s voice was rough as sandpaper, his face etched with worry lines deeper than the fjords he called home. “Elin,” he began, his eyes heavy, “There's something…” He faltered, unable to translate the unyielding weight on his soul, a storm brewing in his chest that threatened to spill over into words. 

“Something?” I prodded, my curiosity laced with an unsettling familiarity. Something was off about this visit, too deliberate and filled with unspoken truths that hovered like fog between us.  It felt less a call for information and more like a plea echoing the emptiness inside of me.

He took a shaky breath. “Anders confessed.”

His words hung in the air, heavy and sharp as the winter wind whistling outside the cabin window. My brows furrowed. "Confessed?" I repeated, the word sounding foreign on my lips. 

Mikael nodded, his face now pale under the flickering light of the oil lamp. "He said…” he hesitated again, "he remembered. He saw something... like a vision." His eyes locked on mine, conveying an unsettling mix of fear and sorrow. “A dream,” he whispered, as if sharing some secret, forbidden truth only shared by the deepest corners of his soul.

The weight of those words fell upon me like a stone in my stomach. A dream? How could that be possible?  Anders had been so adamant, dismissive, refusing to answer even basic questions about the deceased’s past. 

“He spoke of visions,” Mikael repeated, this time with a hint of desperation lacing his voice. "Of something...something connected to the lighthouse." His words, usually measured and calm, now crackled with urgency, begging for confirmation, urging me towards an unknown direction. “The clock tower,” he said quietly, breaking the silence that had settled between us like a thick fog on a cold night in Lofoten. 

His gaze lingered on the weathered picture frame on my desk, its ornate detail depicting a young woman standing proudly under a full moon.  It was Anders' mother’s portrait. We were close to uncovering this truth, but I felt a chill settle over me like an icy hand. Something had changed. Had he truly seen something? Or were these the echoes of something else entirely? 

I searched his eyes for confirmation, trying to see if they held any new revelation or merely reflected the swirling confusion that threatened to engulf my own mind. But I saw nothing there but a shared sense of dread – an unspoken truth, too terrifying to speak aloud.   

“There’s more,” Mikael murmured, his voice barely audible above the rhythmic crash of waves against the shore outside the cabin window. “He spoke about a figure...” He trailed off as if suddenly afraid to continue. 


His words sparked something deep within me, igniting a burning curiosity that refused to be quenched.  I glanced at the worn photograph again – was it just an innocent portrait or a gateway to some deeper secret I wasn’t sure how to decipher? The lighthouse tower loomed large in my mind: a silent sentinel of secrets and forgotten whispers.

“What did he say?” I finally asked, unable to hold back the urgency bubbling within me. It was time for truth, no matter how uncomfortable or unwelcome it might be.


A cold wind whipped through the cabin, rattling the windowpanes.  Mikael shook his head, a strange, tired smile playing on his lips. He leaned forward and whispered in my ear, “He said the woman appeared as if out of thin air.” His voice dropped to a mere murmur, barely audible above the howling winds that seemed to be echoing his unspoken fears. 


A flicker of fear coursed through me – what had he seen?  What did it mean for Anders' confession and for this unfolding mystery? I looked at him, desperately hoping for a glimpse of truth - a signpost pointing to the path forward in this dark fog of uncertainty.

“Did she...” I began, unable to form coherent words amidst the mounting dread that threatened to engulf me.


Mikael cleared his throat, and with his gaze fixed on the weathered photo of Anders’ mother, he spoke, “Yes,” he whispered, his voice barely audible over the wind's relentless howl.
