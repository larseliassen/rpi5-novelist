# Chapter 13

Mikael Larsen's voice was heavy with something unyielding, like stone sinking in seawater. He had arrived at Elin Johansen’s doorstep, his weathered face framed by the grey of a late winter sky that mirrored the storm brewing within her own mind. “She told me,” he began, but then stopped, as if choosing a word from a forgotten lexicon. "She said you know," he finished, eyes filled with years of experience navigating the treacherous waters of truth.

The words hung in the air, heavy and stark before their meaning seeped into her consciousness. The red herring. It had been there all along, lurking beneath the shimmering surface like an old man’s forgotten promise: a woman in white, his sister's ghost whispering from the depths. 

"She," Elin repeated, her own voice trembling.  The weight of that admission pressed against her, constricting her breath and turning the room into a cage of flickering memories. "I thought… I thought I understood.” Her mind raced back to the night she had found the weathered wooden box in Fjerdesund. The mermaid on its lid seemed to mock her with a cryptic grin as if it held the key to some forbidden knowledge; yet another red herring leading to a dead end.

"The lighthouse blueprint," Mikael began, his voice low and resigned like the creaking groan of an aging ship's hull. "A puzzle piece she had hidden away." He moved towards her desk, the worn leather of his gloves whispering against the polished wood as he reached for a file with a faded image of Langesund fyr’s lighthouse tower. “The same one she showed me.”  He continued to speak while Elin’s eyes lingered on the sketch, a ghost of something familiar flickering in her mind.

"This isn't just about Anders,” Mikael said, his voice now strained with sorrow and despair. "There was a connection – a thread leading back further than anyone could have imagined. She found a letter.” 

He unfolded a photograph tucked inside the file, its edges softened by time and the salt-laden sea breeze that whipped through the open window.  The image showed a woman in white standing beside a rickety pier; her face obscured by the rising fog. The figure was almost ethereal, an echo of a forgotten dream. Yet it seemed to pulsate with a hidden meaning, as if the photograph itself held the weight of an unspoken truth.

Elin's eyes flickered from the photo to Mikael’s face – his weathered features etched with sorrow and a quiet desperation that mirrored her own unease. The air grew colder, a subtle shift in the humdrum silence of their shared office. “And what did this woman tell you?” she asked, each word a small, sharp shard piercing the frozen silence.

“She didn't tell me," Mikael replied finally.  His eyes met hers with an urgency born of sorrow and guilt. “But,” he added quietly, "she knew." 


He leaned back in his chair, letting out a sigh as if burdened by unspoken secrets. "There was another box. Found in the old captain's cabin. Just like yours.” The words came out of his mouth like a whisper, almost lost to the rising wind that whipped around their office like a phantom limb. 

"This one…” he said with a tremble, “It held… something more than whispers."
  
His eyes closed, and for a moment, all was silent except the faint hum of the aging electricity grid powering their little haven of words and secrets. 


“The woman," Mikael whispered finally, "she was the key.” A chilling realization dawned on Elin's face. 

It was almost as if she were standing at the edge of a cliff, peering into an abyss that held both hope and dread in equal measure. The weight of it all settled heavy on her chest – each beat echoing in the empty silence of their shared space.  She looked back at the file; the mermaid’s image seemed to mock her with its unwavering gaze as if offering a twisted reflection of her own anxieties. 
"What did she say?" she whispered, barely audible through the biting wind that howled outside.


“About the box,” Mikael said.
