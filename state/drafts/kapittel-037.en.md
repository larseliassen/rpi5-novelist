## Chapter 37

The air hung heavy in the director's office, thick with the scent of salt and something else – an oily undercurrent that clung to the walls like clinging moss.  Elin ran a hand over her tired eyes as she stared at the photo clutched in her hand, the edges worn and faded.  A grainy image of Anders Larsen stood out against the stark white of the office wall: a stoic man with an unsettling intensity behind his wide-set eyes. Mikael had brought her the picture – they'd met for coffee at the harbor cafe, its aroma of freshly baked cinnamon buns doing little to ease the grimness in their eyes.  She’d spoken to him about his growing unease, how the lines between reality and the visions were blurring more with each passing day. He hadn't shared much more than a strained look as he left, a silent promise hanging heavy in the air.

The director, Olsen, walked up to her, his voice rough like sandpaper on wood. "They say he’s gone rogue," he sighed, gesturing towards the photo with an almost weary hand. 

"Gone where?" Elin asked.  She knew it was pointless asking about locations – a man who lived in whispers and fog could vanish as easily as he appeared. It had taken her days to piece together the fragments of his life. She’d seen something flicker across the lines on his face, an old wound that refused to heal.

"Gone," Olsen repeated slowly, watching the way she took in the picture. "He's left the office. Said it was 'not worth the effort.'"  His gaze dropped, and he turned away. 


"What about your note?" Elin asked. Her heart pounded against her ribs like a trapped bird trying to escape its cage.

Olsen looked up then, his eyes sharp and focused as if seeing through her very soul. “I’ve reviewed the notes," he said flatly. "There's been a discrepancy." 


His words felt charged with an unspoken accusation – as if he held a secret, waiting for them both to unlock it. He didn't elaborate on his suspicions; instead, he took another step closer, the air between them crackling with anticipation.

"Something about a man claiming ownership of the camera footage," Olsen continued, tilting the photo in his hand like a talisman, "A fisherman named... Olaf. Said he had 'something to say'."


Elin’s breath hitched; the name rang familiar, echoing through the recesses of her memory like an old song played on a cracked record. It was a man whose face she couldn't quite recall, but his eyes… those were etched with the same haunted despair as Anders'.

"Olaf," Olsen murmured again, dropping the photo back to the table. “It's all connected."


She stood there for a long moment, absorbing everything; the director’s hushed voice like a silent snowfall in an empty room. What she saw was not just an admission – it felt like the beginning of unraveling a knot that had been tightening around her chest since day one.

The office door opened with a soft click, and a tall man stepped into the room, his posture tense as if he held something dangerous within. It was Johan, Olaf's assistant - a quiet presence with eyes that looked too young for such a grave responsibility. 

"Mr. Olsen has asked me to deliver this," Johan said quietly, holding out a small envelope, its weight feeling heavy in the silence. "He thought you might be interested."


Elin's gaze drifted towards the envelope, curiosity clashing with apprehension. Inside it lay a single sheet of paper, folded neatly and adorned with a faded stamp – Olaf’s official signature.  As she unfolded it slowly, her breath hitched. 
    *It was a message.*

"There's no way," Elin whispered, the words barely escaping her lips. “This can't be true.”


The photo in Olsen's hand seemed to blink – as if trying to hold on to something fleeting.  Elin’s eyes drifted down to the letter; a name written in elegant script – one that sent shivers down her spine. 



"Anders Larsen," it read. "He knows more than you think."
