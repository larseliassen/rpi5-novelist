## Chapter 24

A chill wind, biting and sharp, whipped off the fjords and sliced into Elin's face as she stepped out of the director's office. The salty air stung her cheeks, carrying with it the scent of pine and something wilder, something ancient.  A sense of unease clung to her like a damp sheet.

The note from the director lay on his desk, its paper crisp and white despite the lingering salt residue staining every corner. "Witness account: Anna-Lára Johansen," he'd written in short, angular strokes. It was a name almost synonymous with fear, whispers of an apparition, a specter that haunts these very shores. A thread finally connecting two seemingly disparate lines of investigation;  the dead man's past and the woman from the docks. 

She walked briskly down to the waterfront, her gaze fixed on the horizon where swirling clouds mirrored the churning sea. The ferry was anchored just beyond the harbor entrance, its bow a beacon against the encroaching dusk. A figure stood at its edge, shrouded in the dimness of fading light – tall and lean, with a raven's cloak hanging about their frame like a restless shadow.  The man didn't speak; but she knew him now, felt his presence as surely as she felt the grip of the sea wind on her face.

Mikael Larsen. 

His eyes, though hidden by shadows, held an intensity that pierced through the gloom. "You have discovered something," he said, his voice like rough stones rolling together in a distant storm.  "Something that demands our attention." 


He held up a faded photograph -  an image of Ingrid Larsen as she'd been during her prime; younger, vibrant, and radiant with life. The woman's eyes held an unsettling intensity, almost devoid of light, like the depths of a frozen lake after a winter storm. It seemed as if the very air around her was turning to ice, but it was not just Ingrid’s spirit in that image; something else lurked beneath, clinging to a forgotten echo from another life. 

"The woman," Mikael said, his voice almost inaudible, a whisper of wind through dry leaves. "She's real, as real as the storm.” His eyes, like pools reflecting moonlight, now seemed to harbor some hidden truth, something far more potent than mere witness accounts.

He added, with an unspoken weight on his shoulders: "She’s also connected to him."  The man's voice wavered for a moment before continuing, “to the dead man. The one we found..." 


The director's words echoed in her mind: "Witness account." An apparition at Fjerdesund; a young woman claiming to have seen the deceased near the docks after dark, but only as a fleeting glimpse, barely perceptible against the sea mist and the swirling wind. It had been a fleeting chance encounter— yet,  it now felt like a crack in the foundation of her investigation, an unexpected fissure opening up beneath her feet.

 "She said," he continued, his gaze piercing the thick fog that clung to the edge of consciousness, "she saw him at dusk.” 


His words hung heavy in the air, a chilling truth that chilled Elin to her core. “What does this mean?" she asked, her voice thin and shaky, unable to quell the rising tide of unease gnawing at her gut.

Mikael’s eyes met hers, his gaze unwavering and piercing. "It means we're closer to the answer," he said, his voice laced with a strange confidence. He gestured towards the harbor entrance. “The truth is waiting."


As Elin followed him through the mist-shrouded docks, the camera crew’s presence felt more menacing than before; their silence was like a shroud of unspoken secrets. She noticed an uncanny calmness in these men – their composure seemed to defy the turmoil brewing within her own soul. 



The wind picked up around them, whispering tales of forgotten sorrows and long-buried truths. A sudden gust whipped at Elin's coat as she glanced towards the sea, searching for a sign, a spark of understanding.
