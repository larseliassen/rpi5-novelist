## Chapter 14

Elin’s fingers dug into the worn leather of her phone, its screen glowing in the dimly lit cabin. The call from Mikael had been brief, his voice a low rumble that rattled through her like a sudden gust of wind. He'd said he had something to show her.  She pressed the button for "call back," letting it hang in silence before pulling out a pen and scribbling down a few notes: 
“Mikael has a box? At home?”

The weight of unanswered questions hung heavy on her shoulders as she waited. The storm raged outside, its furious rhythm mirroring the turmoil inside. Every rustle of wind seemed to carry whispers of secrets, each gust threatening to blow away the fragile thread that held her investigation together. This was not just about a dead man or a missing camera; it was something more, something that felt woven into the very fabric of Fjerdesund and whispered in the mournful cries of the gulls circling above.

She'd been through countless police stations, interviewed hundreds of people with faces etched by the same melancholy as she had witnessed on her own. The answers remained elusive like phantoms dancing behind a veil. It was the fog, that seemed to cling to everything; clinging to her doubts, fueling her anxiety. 

Now, Mikael's arrival felt like a ray of light piercing through the oppressive grey clouds. He stood there at the doorway of her small apartment in the old fishermen’s village, his face etched with worry and weariness.  His presence was almost tangible - an unexpected warmth amidst the harsh chill that seemed to permeate every corner of the city. 

 “Elin,” he started, his voice rough as sandpaper on wood. “I have something I need you to see."


He held a small wooden box in his calloused hands, its edges worn smooth by years of use.  The lid was raised, but it wasn't open, and there was no sign of dust. It looked as if the contents were waiting for her touch.

"Is... is that from the captain?" she asked. The question hung heavy in the air, a tangible weight dragging down her confidence. 
She picked up the box with trepidation. Inside, nestled amongst worn fabric, lay an image of a mermaid playing flute and surrounded by waves and stars –  an old ship whistle, its metal body rusted but still humming faintly with some hidden energy. 

The moment she touched it, a jolt ran through her, like static electricity crackling across her skin. There was something in the air around it, something almost tangible, as if the very box itself held a whisper of the past; a story left untold. She traced the intricate carvings on its surface with trembling fingers, each symbol whispering a silent truth she could not yet understand.

Her eyes widened and her pulse quickened. It was... familiar. Almost too familiar to be real. The image seemed to echo within her very soul, stirring long-buried memories of forgotten dreams, buried in the depths of her subconscious. Was it an echo from a past life? A memory that was never truly lost, yet always lingered just out of reach?  And then she saw it - a tiny inscription carved on the back of the box: "For my dearest Astrid." 

Her breath caught in her throat as realization washed over her like a tidal wave. The image resonated with a sense of dread; an echo from somewhere deep within, stirring something long buried beneath the surface. There was something in that whistle's design, a certain rhythm and grace, almost too perfect to be mere coincidence. 

“Mikael,” she breathed, her voice hoarse. “Why?”  She knew why but couldn’t bear to hear it spoken aloud. It was an answer waiting to be discovered, a truth hidden beneath layers of doubt. But Mikael didn't look at the box and speak. He just stepped back, his eyes wide with a flicker of recognition. 

"Come with me," he said, his voice low but urgent. 
He led her down a dark alleyway behind their houses, past an abandoned shipyard where the rusting hulk of a forgotten boat remained frozen in time.  The old town seemed to shrink around them, the fog clinging to the brick walls like ghostly tendrils.


"We have more than answers," he said. "But something dangerous."
