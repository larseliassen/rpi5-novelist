##  Chapter 29: A False Trail

The director’s office reeked of seawater and old secrets. His voice, gruff as gravel, had been echoing through Elin's skull since she’d entered the space. It wasn't his usual tone, this hushed urgency. He seemed to be holding something back, but he wouldn't elaborate. 

“Anders Larsen,” he said finally, “He claims it was a dream – that an old ship captain in white gave him visions of a woman.” The director’s words were clipped, almost dismissive, his gaze fixed on the floor.  A wave of confusion crashed over Elin. Was he talking about Anders’ memories? About the man's past? Had Anders confessed this at all?

Mikael had said something similar during their investigation – a fleeting mention of “visions” and "the woman in white." But it hadn't been clear, just an echo in her mind. The director's sudden shift was unnerving.  "He claims that he was visited by this... figure," the director continued, his voice low but sharp as a shard. "A kind of spectral presence. A ghostly messenger.”

The air hung thick and heavy, charged with unspoken tension. 

"But Anders," the director muttered under his breath, leaning back in his chair. "Anders' visions don't fit." He was speaking now as if to himself. “There's something off about this whole thing."

A flicker of doubt crossed Elin’s face, a sudden flash of unease. The director had mentioned an inconsistency earlier – some small detail she hadn't been able to pinpoint - that he couldn’t quite explain yet. This was it, the missing piece, and now it was staring back at them from behind the opaque silence.  He finally looked up, a glint in his eye. “The lighthouse,” he said. His voice suddenly held an ominous weight. 

“A few days ago, we received some strange information.”  Elin’s heart hammered against her ribs as the director's eyes met hers. "We found traces of a visit at the Værnes Lighthouse that day." He paused for a moment before continuing. “And someone sent in a photo of Ingrid Larsen with the same time-stamp as when they witnessed Anders’ presence by the docks.”

“Ingrid was close to Anders,” Elin muttered, her voice barely above a whisper. It didn't sound like a confirmation - more like an accusation. 

"She wasn’t just a witness," the director said, his gaze unwavering. "There's something about her that suggests she was involved." A wave of anger washed over him as if a part of himself had been shattered in front of them.  He looked at the photo on the table and then back at Elin, the corner of his mouth twitching as he spoke.

"It’s a red herring.” The director's eyes narrowed, but now they held something softer, more understanding than before. “The camera footage is missing,” he murmured, and there was that feeling again - an unease.  “We should have found it sooner. But we didn’t.” He looked at Elin, his gaze filled with a weariness that seemed to stretch back years. "She must be lying."

A cold wave of realization washed over Elin as she realized the full scope of what had been misconstrued all along. The missing camera footage wasn't just a coincidence - it was the director’s way of creating a false trail, leading them down an endless path. 

“What about Anders?” she asked, pushing back from the table and rising to her feet, “If he is telling the truth…” 

The director’s eyes met hers for a long moment before he slowly nodded. “Someone's trying to hide something,” he murmured.  He stood up, his shoulders slumped as if burdened by some unseen weight. "We need to find out what.”


 The air in the office seemed suddenly thick and stagnant. 

“Who else is involved?” Elin asked, her voice tight with a mix of fear and resolve. “And why would they cover it up?"  She knew there was more to learn, but she couldn’t help but feel as if she was already tumbling into an abyss of questions that had no answers.
