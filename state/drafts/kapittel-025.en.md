## Chapter 25

The director’s voice crackled through the intercom, barely audible beneath the howling wind and the relentless crashing of waves against the docks at Fjerdesund. “Elin…” He began, then sighed heavily. "There's something else." His tone was subdued, a distinct shift from his usual assuredness, as if a weight had settled upon him just as heavy as the rain that pounded down on the roof of the director’s office. 

He spoke in short bursts, each more confusing than the last. At first, it was a detail about the camera footage missing; now, he said:  "The witness, she wasn't alone."  There had been another woman present when the man was last seen alive by the docks – an older woman, seemingly unperturbed by what seemed like an escalating disaster of a situation. 

Elin found herself staring at the old photo in her hands, its edges worn and faded. It showed Ingrid Larsen's face, etched with a serenity that felt jarringly out of place against the backdrop of the chaotic scene she was now piecing together. She flipped it over and over in her palm, searching for some hidden significance, some forgotten key to unlocking this impossible enigma. 

"The camera crew," the director’s voice rasped again as he leaned back into his chair, "They said they saw a woman – young, blonde – walking with Anders." He paused and added: “She looked like she was heading in the opposite direction.” 

Elin frowned.  “Anders wasn't close to Ingrid Larsen,” she stated quietly. This information came from Mikael, who had been tracking down potential leads all week long; it was a lead that felt almost surreal given their initial meeting at Skrogvær where Anders’ evasiveness seemed so tangible and real. 

The director coughed awkwardly before continuing. "We need to check the footage.” He said this as if waiting for something, a moment of anticipation, a pause in his own internal monologue.


It was like watching an unraveling clock; every minute felt long, heavy with uncertainty. A wave of doubt washed over her, replacing the confidence she’d clung to throughout the investigation. 

"The witness' description..." The director's voice trailed off again as he stared down at a document on his desk, then back up at Elin. "We should be questioning her." 
She nodded stiffly and felt something stir inside of her – fear perhaps? Or an unsettling feeling that the truth was being masked by layers upon layers of lies and deceit.

It was like staring into a reflection, but this wasn't her; it was an image that haunted the edges of her vision. The woman in the photo seemed to be laughing or crying, something about how she looked made Elin feel strangely out of sync with reality itself.

 "Mikael,” he continued. “He has been quiet for hours – I think he is ready to talk."

Elin didn't say anything for a moment, just stared at the director who seemed to shrink into his own skin as if his thoughts were trying to escape through his fingertips. 


The wind howled past her as she hurried down the cobblestone alleyway towards Mikael’s office – the only path that would lead her straight toward answers. It felt like a test, one that might leave her drowning in the chaos, unable to navigate it with the same certainty she once possessed.  A new question formed itself within her mind: was there more here than simply a man's past and someone else’s secret?
