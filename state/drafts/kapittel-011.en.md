## Chapter 11:  The Lighthouse's Secrets 


The salty air clung to Elin Johansen like a shroud, even in the sheltered confines of the police station. The smell permeated everything – fish bones and old wood from years of being swept by sea spray. It reminded her of Langesund fyr, its pungent aroma clinging to the pages of the fire warden's diary she held under the harsh fluorescent light. 

Days had melted into nights since the body was found. The initial shock wore off, replaced with a relentless churning in her stomach, an unsettling feeling that something wasn’t quite right.  She felt like she was standing on the edge of a vastness, unsure of how to proceed, how to navigate this intricate web of lies and half-truths. 

Her gaze lingered on Anders Bjørnstad's face in the worn photograph – his eyes as empty as the sea after a storm, yet somehow brimming with hidden depths that he kept locked away from her.  He had refused to answer questions; each encounter was filled with an air of evasion and suspicion. The camera found in his bag held a vital piece: not a picture but a video file of the dead man's last moments, seemingly lost somewhere along the journey back to the mainland. 

The director’s notes kept echoing in her mind – “Dig up a clue from an earlier chapter and give it new meaning.” She looked down at the photograph again,  trying to make sense of its significance –  the worn leather strap around Anders Bjørnstad's wrist, something that had caught her eye during their first meeting. 

She recalled that day: the man’s stillness in his wake as she interviewed him about the incident, how he wouldn't meet her gaze for long, as if he was trying to hide a storm within himself. The old photographs of him, too, were strangely unsettling; almost a caricature of the real person she now had to apprehend.  His image was like a phantom limb – the memories and images that refused to be forgotten.

Her eyes fell on a small box tucked away in a corner of her desk, a relic from Anders Bjørnstad’s investigation. It contained an old ship whistle, rusty but emitting a faint hum through its intricate carvings - a mermaid playing flute and surrounded by waves and stars –  a curious image that seemed to beckon out of the darkness. The box itself was crafted in weathered wood, rough-hewn, yet it felt strangely comforting; as if it had been sculpted by sea winds and salty rain for centuries.

Suddenly, she remembered what Anders Bjørnstad’s words meant - "Lighthouses do not always guide you where you need to go," he said on that day when the sea was churning with a wild fury. His words echoed in her mind as the realization hit – his face had been etched into her memories like a ghost, leaving an unsettling presence in this small, cluttered office.

A knock at the door interrupted her thoughts. It was  Mikael Larsen – Anders’ childhood friend, now living on the islands with his family for years after the incident.

“Elin,” he started cautiously, “I found something... I think it might be a part of your investigation."  He held a small, black box from his pocket as if to protect its contents from the world outside. "My wife said Anders was always drawing things - intricate maps and sketches, even when we were kids.” 

As he opened the small wooden box, Elin saw a set of blueprints for an underwater construction project. The designs depicted bizarre structures resembling miniature versions of lighthouses - yet these structures had nothing to do with anything she knew about coastal architecture or seafaring.  There was something more – a hidden layer to them that only hinted at an old secret, an answer waiting for her. 


“Lighthouses are silent guards," Mikael murmured as he handed the box back to Elin, "And some secrets have no light to guide us.” He left without another word.
