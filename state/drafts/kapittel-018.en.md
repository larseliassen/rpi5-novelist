## Chapter 18

The director's note was clear: let a minor character behave suspiciously.  It made no sense, of course. A single-minded, meticulous police officer like Elin Johansen could easily track down any anomaly that arose— or even create one if need be. Yet, there he stood, with his hand on the worn railing of the ferry's deck as it rolled through the misty waters towards Fjerdesund, and something about him felt... off. 

Mikael Larsen had arrived at the dock earlier than expected. His usual sharp-eyed gaze, usually a beacon in the bustling chaos of Fjerdesund harbor, seemed to flicker with an internal war, his features frozen between impatience and unease. He hadn’t been able to wait for Elin's confirmation that Anders Bjørnstad had indeed confessed about the dead man's past as he'd insisted upon earlier this afternoon.  Instead, he'd simply taken a seat on the ferry's upper deck, his gaze flitting around, occasionally landing on the weathered faces of locals who seemed to be watching him with quiet judgment.

"Anders claimed he had seen visions, visions connected to the lighthouse and the woman in that portrait," Mikael began when Elin boarded, his voice thick and low as if he’d choked on something while speaking. His hand went automatically to a pocket watch hidden beneath his coat.  “He says she spoke to him in dreams.” He said it like a tired accusation, like a burden he was trying to bear alone.

Elin's eyes narrowed at the cryptic statement. "The visions?” 

"Yes,” Mikael said, pushing his glasses back up onto his nose as if adjusting them for emphasis. “He’s haunted by her." A shadow flickered across his face, quick and fleeting before it vanished. “She was a part of his life.”


Mikael's words hung in the air like smoke. It felt too real, too deliberate. As she studied the man whose features were etched in both grief and something else - perhaps fear –  a sense of dread settled over Elin. 

"We need to find these visions," Elin said, her voice a tight whisper despite her best efforts. She watched as his lips thinned into an unsettling grimace, then he nodded slowly, a silent acknowledgement of the shared purpose.

“I’ll help,” he said before turning towards the door, a hint of something colder creeping across his features:  a calculated coldness that made Elin's pulse quicken in her chest. 

The ferry journey was filled with an eerie stillness as they approached the shore of Fjerdesund, the silence broken only by their hurried steps on the worn wooden deck as the ship pulled towards the dock. The sea seemed to shimmer under a veil of mist, casting long shadows across the land, and as Mikael lingered near the railing, his eyes never left the water's edge. 

"We'll need more than just Anders’ confessions," Elin murmured, studying Mikael. 

He turned, his gaze meeting hers for a fraction of a moment before he averted it, his lips twitching as if holding back an unspoken truth.  “The man who found him,” he said, voice low and measured, “He… He saw something.” His words were clipped, leaving an unsettling silence in their wake.

And then, with a slow sweep of his hand, he pointed to the sky - a wisp of smoke rising from the distance, trailing like an elusive phantom. 


"The lighthouse," he said quietly, his eyes wide, as if seeing something beyond her perception.  

A shiver ran down Elin's spine. The image was too vivid; so strange, yet familiar in its hauntingly uncanny way - a feeling that tugged at the very edges of her consciousness and left her standing on the precipice of an unraveling truth. 


"The smoke?" she echoed, her gaze fixed on him, searching for some deeper meaning behind his cryptic words, something hidden in the shadows of his eyes that refused to be unveiled.  A sudden realization dawned upon her: this wasn't just about Anders Bjørnstad anymore; it was about something bigger, something more sinister lurking beneath the surface - a secret that even she hadn’t yet fully grasped.
