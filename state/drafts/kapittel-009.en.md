## Chapter 9: The Ship's Whistle

The salt-laced wind whipped Elin’s hair across her face as she stood at the edge of the pier, watching the ferry sail back into the bustling harbor. Its presence, even from afar, was jarringly loud amidst the quiet, echoing silence that seemed to settle over everything after a storm like this one. The Fjerdesund had been pounded by relentless waves for two days now.  She’d found nothing in her investigation of Anders Bjørnstad, only a lingering unease and a strange, metallic scent clinging to the air around him – like rust or old blood. It was as if the man himself was shrouded in a film of something unsayable.

The ferry's arrival brought more witnesses, faces etched with a similar fear she had begun to sense. The weight of it all pressed on her chest. 

She found herself rereading the fire warden’s diary again, hoping for an answer within its weathered pages. It felt like a trap; every page was a tangled web of conflicting memories and half-told stories from men who were supposed to be in charge of protecting these waters but often forgot their duty in the face of despair.  The man's words seemed to mock her with his casual detachment, as if he hadn’t been there. "He did it," Ingrid Larsen had said again and again, her eyes shadowed, unable to explain what she meant. "But he won't speak about it." It was a haunting refrain that followed her everywhere – in the wind whistling through the skeletal branches of the old pine forest on the island, in the swirling snow that seemed to cling to everything like memory itself.  The man's silence wasn’t his own; it was something larger and more dangerous than anything she had encountered before.

Days ago, while searching for the missing camera footage near the site where the body had been found, she'd stumbled upon a small box tucked into the corner of a cabin wall. Inside, nestled amongst worn fabric, lay an old ship whistle – rusted but still humming faintly with some hidden energy. As if drawn by it, her fingers had traced the intricate carvings on its surface: a mermaid playing flute and surrounded by waves and stars. The image resonated with something deep inside her – a feeling of both hope and dread.

The box was no ordinary find; it belonged to the ship's former captain, who had taken to the sea in his last years.  He was known for disappearing into thin air, leaving behind only rumors and an uncanny sense that he still remained at sea—never truly gone, but forever wandering, a ghost trapped by unseen forces. He had been on the island during the time of tragedy, his body never recovered after the storm. This could be their connection – something in the box resonated with her own experiences, drawing a line between the present and the past. 

She knew it was an old man’s story, but she couldn't shake off this feeling. Something wasn't right. She had seen enough; time wasn't on her side anymore. The camera footage – if she could ever find it—was now more than just evidence. It was a piece of the puzzle that would lead to closure. This case was no longer simply about finding answers, but about understanding their true nature: life, death and the mysteries they held. A new wave crashed against the pier; the wind whipped it into her face, and she wondered if this case could ever truly end? 

As a faint, metallic whistle pierced the silence of the night, the air crackled with an unspoken threat. It seemed as if someone was watching. Someone in their thoughts knew everything.  And now, Elin would find out who, where, how. Her investigation had shifted from finding answers to uncovering secrets hidden for decades—secrets about a man and his connection to the island’s history.
