## Chapter 22

The director's office was a tomb of secrets, each surface echoing the weight of untold stories. A thick, gray fog hung like damp curtains, obscuring even the meager light that filtered through the grimy windows.  Elin’s gaze drifted from the worn desk to the floor, her eyes finding their way to a loose thread on a threadbare chair cushion. It was there, a single strand of faded orange, that she found it: a tiny, faded picture tucked within the upholstery seam. The edges were frayed and cracked, but the image stared out at her with an eerie intensity. A younger Ingrid Larsen stood against the backdrop of a rocky cliffside overlooking the shimmering blue water - stark white waves crashing against the shore as if reaching for the sky.  Beneath her, two figures – perhaps lovers in happier times, perhaps just childhood friends – gazed into the distance, their eyes filled with unspoken longing. 

She pulled the picture from the chair and turned it over and over in her hands, tracing the faded lines of Ingrid's face as she had done so many times before. Something about the image resonated deeper than a simple memory: an echo of forgotten dreams.  It made her want to stand on that same cliffside, let the wind whisper through her hair while memories danced in the swirling mist.  

Mikael, at the director's request, had been showing her photos of Anders Bjørnstad - not in his current state but as a younger man, perhaps twenty years old or so. But this was different. This image held an almost tangible sorrow, a palpable weight that seemed to cling to it like a ghostly shroud. There was something about Ingrid’s photo that matched what she felt for Anders; an aching sense of loss, as if the past and present were intertwined in some secret knot. 

“He’s not just a man who disappeared into the ocean,” Mikael confessed, his eyes meeting hers with an almost desperate intensity. “He holds something deep within him.”

The Director's phone rang from the desk. A voice on the other end – clipped and curt, yet laced with urgency.  "Elin Johansen," he said, his face etched with grim purpose. "There's a development. The witness saw Anders again last night. On the ferry, by the docks.”

A shiver ran through Elin, an icy hand sliding down her spine like a forgotten memory of fear. She could barely breathe, a knot forming in her stomach as she pieced together the fragmented pieces of the puzzle.  "The witness," she said, her voice a mere whisper against the oppressive silence that seemed to cling to the room. "What was he doing?"

The director's reply was an abrupt click on the phone – no time for questions or answers. “He's been watching him,” he continued. 
“We need to move him before he draws suspicion. We’re running out of time."


"But where is he going?” she asked, desperately seeking any sliver of light in the encroaching darkness of uncertainty.

The director shook his head – a gesture that echoed with a silent despair – and simply said, "Don't go to Fjerdesund.” 
“He knows you’ll find him,” the Director warned.  
 


 Elin clutched her notebook like a lifeline as if there were a secret answer hidden in its worn pages. It was then she understood; Anders had been watching them, waiting for this moment - and his escape plan had already begun. She knew that something had irrevocably shifted the balance of power in this case – an unsettling realization, yet one she couldn't quite grasp.
