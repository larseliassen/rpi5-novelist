## Chapter 28

Elin woke to a discordant symphony of wind and rain against the windowpanes. Outside, the storm raged, a tangible force that seemed to claw at the very bones of her apartment. The director's note lay on her desk – short, stark: “ Anders Larsen. Deadline. No further communication.”

She hadn’t spoken with Mikael in several days, their late-night calls becoming infrequent and curt as if they were both carrying secrets.  The weight of his silence was a tangible thing now, pressing down on her chest like the rising tide. He wouldn't reveal anything to her without a reason. A sense of foreboding crawled up her spine, an unsettling premonition of something sinister brewing beneath the icy surface of their investigation. 

She pulled out her phone and dialed his number once more.  "Mikael?" she said into the receiver, though his name felt foreign in her own mouth. The line crackled as he answered. "Elin," he rasped, his voice barely above a whisper. "Something's happening."


He didn’t elaborate further but that was all the indication she needed. 

At the Fjerdesund site,  the captain's cryptic message echoed back to her: “The mermaid sings.” The wind howled like a banshee as she stood on the rickety dock, the rain stinging at her face. It had been days since they found his box; days since the phone call from Mikael that sent him scrambling away with a storm brewing in his eyes. 

She needed answers. And somewhere, amidst the swirling sea of doubt and uncertainty, was the key to unlocking it all.  The missing camera footage, a witness account, a connection between Anders' visions – these were threads she desperately clung to as she navigated this treacherous maze of unanswered questions. The clock ticked away, each second adding another layer of pressure. She had no choice; she needed to be relentless, like the waves crashing against the shore with unrelenting fury. 

Suddenly, a flash of crimson in the distance caught her eye. A car was pulling up, its headlights slicing through the heavy fog that clung to the harbor’s edges.  She rushed towards it, adrenaline pumping through her veins, and found Mikael standing near the driver's seat – he looked as though he had barely slept. "The car," he said, his voice urgent as if they were both being chased by a phantom predator, "It was here."

His eyes met hers; she saw that something was different tonight - the same cold intensity but with an undercurrent of fear. He'd been holding back until now? Why? 

He stepped back from the car and held out a photograph. It showed Ingrid Larsen – her face pale, yet serene in the flickering light. "She said she saw him," he murmured as if reading a dark prophecy written in watercolors, “Near the docks." He looked at Elin's face; his gaze lingered a moment too long before looking away. 

Elin took the picture, a cold feeling blooming in her chest. The photograph felt charged with an energy that mirrored the storm raging around them. The scene was almost tangible – Ingrid's smile, her serene composure. It was like peering into another life, one not lived within these grey, rainy streets but a world of endless possibility and a stark silence. 


She stared at it, trying to decipher its meaning.

"I know what he saw," Mikael said, his voice echoing in the vast emptiness of the night. "But I have no idea why."
