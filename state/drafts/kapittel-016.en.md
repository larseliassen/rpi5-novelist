##  Chapter 16: The Clock Tower's Whisper

The Norwegian wind gnawed at Elin’s cheeks as she stood before the dilapidated clock tower in Skrogvær village. It had been years since Mikael brought her here, decades even, but memories of the past seemed to cling to this place like fog. The air, thick with salt and diesel fumes, carried an undercurrent of melancholy.  The village, tucked away on the rocky coast, whispered tales from long-gone fishermen, secrets etched into weathered stones and rusting ship parts. 

Mikael stood beside her, a shadow of his former self, eyes shadowed with fatigue. “He said something about the tower,” he murmured, tapping a worn leather pouch hanging at his waist. "A clockmaker’s legacy.”  

“The clock tower is just a tower," Elin stated, though even she couldn't quite dismiss its weight in her heart. It was a place of memory and echoes, where stories took flight and sometimes found their truth within the rusted gears and chipped paint. 

"He said something about an answer," Mikael repeated. 
“About answers?”  Elin asked, taking a step back as if she were walking through the ruins of a crumbling dream. 

Mikael nodded slowly. "He was looking for his father's diary."

Their eyes met across the gap of the worn-out clock face. A silent understanding passed between them, each unspoken word carrying weight like an old anchor tied to the soul.  The day after finding that box with the mermaid image and a metal taste in her mouth -  like a ghost’s whisper lingering on steel. 

“He was always fascinated by these stories,” Mikael said finally, his voice low. "Legends of ships lost at sea and men cursed for their sins.” He pointed to a nearby wooden sign etched with faded words: 'Skrogvær Maritime Museum'. "Maybe some answers are meant to be buried." 

Elin followed his gaze, her eyes drawn to the inscription - ‘Lost Souls Remembered’.  It was as if each carved letter had been forged in a cauldron of sorrow, its magic imbued with an undercurrent of lost lives and silent screams. She ran her fingers over the rough wood of a nearby bench, feeling for a hidden secret buried beneath years of neglect. 

“What about the woman in white?” she asked, a sudden jolt of fear cutting through their shared history. “The one from his memories? Is that part of his father's legacy too?”  Her voice caught. 

Mikael looked at her, a flicker of something passing across his tired eyes – could it be sadness, or relief? "She's... more than just a memory," he finally said, his gaze drifting to the water as if the memories were slipping away. “There’s something else, something beyond.”  

His words echoed in the sudden silence. The sea seemed to churn and sigh, swirling around them like an ancient secret waiting to be released. 


A sudden bang came from within the museum. It was a rusty sound that reverberated through the air - almost as if a trapped bird was desperately trying to escape its cage. Elin stepped closer. "It's… a clock," she whispered, her eyes widening with something akin to fascination and dread.  The metal gears of an old timepiece ticked loudly and rhythmically. The face was scratched, with hands frozen at a moment in time – as if the world had stopped for just a second before being shattered into pieces. 


Suddenly, it clicked. A memory, not one from Mikael’s past, but her own -  a memory that she’d buried under years of grief and guilt. Her eyes met his again and there was an understanding there – a shared burden, perhaps even something more.

It felt like time itself had slowed down. The wind whipped around them, carrying the scent of brine and old memories. Elin stumbled back as if struck by invisible hands. A truth she’d denied for years now threatened to spill out onto the floor.  She needed answers – from someone, or something - but who could she turn to?


"It's the woman in white," she said softly, her voice barely a murmur. "We need to talk."
