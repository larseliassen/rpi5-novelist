# Chapter 10

The salty air whipped through Elin Johansen's windswept hair as she stood at the docks of Skrogvær, watching the ferry unload its passengers. The day was brisk, the biting frost making the sea spray cling to her cheeks like frosty tears. Every face around her seemed to carry a similar expression of stoic fatigue, their weathered features reflecting the unforgiving winter landscape that stretched out behind them. Even amidst the quiet hum of anticipation for tomorrow's ferry trip to Langesund, Elin felt a gnawing tension in her gut, an unwelcome premonition that this investigation was just starting to scratch the surface of something much deeper than she could have imagined. 

Her gaze drifted towards the battered wooden railing of the dock, then downwards as if drawn by an invisible pull.  On one side of the plank, nestled between stacked fishing crates and a weathered stack of driftwood pallets, sat Anders Bjørnstad, looking more forlorn than he had seemed in her last encounter.  He was hunched over, his bony fingers fiddling with the worn leather strap of a small bag slung over his shoulder. 

It was there that she saw him.  A flash of red and yellow caught her eye - the unmistakable color combination of an official "warning" sign used for dangerous waters in Lofoten-sjøen, hanging off a rusty buoy tied to one side of the dock's edge. The detail wasn't significant enough to make it stand out as anything other than routine – yet something about its presence irked her deeply. 

"Mr Bjørnstad?" she called, her voice echoing in the icy air.  His head snapped up, his eyes meeting hers with a chilling intensity that seemed born from years of isolation, of secrets he refused to divulge. He remained silent for a moment, as if weighing something against an unyielding tide of self-doubt before finally offering a curt nod. "Can I ask you about the camera?"

Her query was met by a brief flicker of recognition in his eyes, followed by a hesitant smile that seemed more like a grimace than genuine mirth. He gestured towards a weathered wooden box tucked inside a corner of his bag.  He didn't speak, yet the way he offered it up to her felt deliberate, almost…accusatory? 

"I…," he began, his voice barely above a whisper, "didn't think it would be so important." His face was unreadable, every wrinkle etched by years of hardship a silent testament to forgotten sorrows. Yet there was something strange about his demeanor; an almost feverish excitement that felt like a dangerous game in progress.

Elin took the box from him – its worn surface cool against her palm. A wave of nausea washed over her; this wasn't simply about locating the missing footage. This was more than just evidence, it was a piece of the puzzle she couldn’t quite grasp, a strange and alluring thread leading to something she could not name but felt deep within her soul.  The image on the box lid – a faded mermaid playing flute amidst churning waves – seemed to pulse with an otherworldly energy that left her breathless. It was like looking into the abyss of her own subconscious - filled with anxieties, fears she hadn't dared to confront until now. 

"There’s more," he rasped, a strange urgency in his voice that broke through the frozen silence. “You know how this is?”  He paused. "It wasn't even me.” He finally looked at her, his eyes blazing with some unknown fire; the flicker of a man forced to betray his own secrets. The look in his eyes was one of regret, yet it carried an undeniable undercurrent of something more sinister—a promise he couldn’t keep, and something she yearned to understand.  


He slipped into the shadows of a nearby building, leaving behind only the faint echo of his words. 

“There's no way back,” he whispered. "No," he repeated, before letting out a guttural laugh that seemed devoid of any humor. “But there is always another story waiting to unfold."
