## Chapter 30:  The Man Who Vanished
   
The director's office felt like a steel coffin, cold and oppressive even as the wind outside howled with icy fury. His notes lay in front of me, stark white against the dark wood table; each word on that page was a hammer blow to my already weary skull.  

“Anders Larsen vanished three days ago,” The director said quietly, his eyes locked on mine. He wore a grey suit that seemed too formal for such a mundane affair— a man in an endless battle with his own anxieties. “He disappeared from his apartment without any trace.”

Mikael had just arrived in my office, and I'd hoped he would bring some comfort, some normalcy to this storm-ridden world.  But even the steady presence of the man’s calm composure seemed to be fighting a losing battle against this case's relentless grip on us both. His face was pale, his eyes hollow, haunted by something he clearly wouldn't speak about. 

“The witnesses saw him near the docks at precisely 10:45 pm,” The director muttered, his gaze lingering for an uncomfortably long moment before returning to me. “He disappeared shortly after that.”  Mikael was silent. I could almost see him fighting the urge to say something— or maybe even confess, a flicker of vulnerability flashing in his eyes.   

The Director cleared his throat, and said: "Anders Larsen's alibi is crumbling faster than we thought it would." 


"Why wouldn’t he just speak?" He finally asked, turning away from me as if the mere act of looking was an assault. "What could there be to hide?” 

The director shook his head slightly. “We don’t have anything concrete.”
He paused, and then continued:  “Anders Larsen is a man with secrets, Elin. Secrets that run deeper than any wave crashing against these shores."


I took a deep breath and glanced at the photos of Anders that Mikael had handed me earlier. The image of Ingrid Larsen in her wedding dress looked like she’d just stepped out of an old movie - her eyes holding a strange mixture of hope, fear, and something else I couldn't quite place.

"The witnesses claim they saw him near the docks again," the director said as if reading my thoughts. “They say he was with… someone.” 


He pointed to a name on one of the photos - Ingrid Larsen's face. It was faded in the photo but her eyes still seemed to hold some kind of weight, like an anchor dragging her down towards something unknown.  "She has been strangely quiet,” The director continued. "Almost as if she knows something... but is afraid."


My mind raced back to Anders’ interview a week ago. It had felt so fleeting; the man was evasive and seemed almost detached. He'd mentioned “someone” in his account, someone who made him feel like he could vanish into thin air. Now I wondered if "someone" was more than just another alias. 

“But why?” The director said as if it was a question posed for the ages.  
Mikael looked at me with an expression that seemed to ask: “Why not.”


I shook my head, unable to formulate a coherent response. "We need answers," I muttered, more to myself than the man across from me. 

He glanced at Mikael, who was still trying to make sense of this case’s sudden turn. He was quiet for a moment, then said: “I think we have something.”



The director's office door swung shut behind us as the storm outside intensified.   A sense of foreboding clung like fog to the air; heavy with unspoken truths and whispered secrets.  We both looked at each other in that instant - two detectives standing on the precipice of a new revelation, staring down into a dark abyss where all we could do was hope that answers would surface from this swirling storm.
