## Chapter 20

A low, guttural rumble echoed through Elin’s bones as she pulled open the heavy door to the director's office at Værnes Police Station. The air within was thick with salt and an undercurrent of something darker – an oppressive weight that pressed in on her like a living thing.  The director, his face etched with lines that looked both weathered and impossibly youthful, awaited her. 

"Elin," he said, his voice carrying the same weariness as the endless Norwegian storms he seemed to embody. "There's been a development." He gestured towards a small table littered with papers, each one bearing the weight of countless hours poured into this case. “I don’t have much time. We need you on the ship.”

She scanned his words; they held a threat as heavy and inescapable as the storm outside that raged for a lifetime in its own right. "What do you mean? The police haven't gotten hold of any leads yet." 
 
"Not to be blunt," the director’s voice was husky with a suppressed tremor, “this is a matter of urgency.” He pushed back his chair, revealing a stark photograph on a glass-covered stand. It showed a weathered figure standing in front of an ominous clock tower.  “The deceased’s body… I want you to find out what happened after he disappeared."

A cold dread washed over her as she stared at the photo. This was something she hadn't anticipated, and yet it felt inevitable. The clock tower; the dead man; the box – all of it felt like a puzzle with pieces that didn’t quite fit but were somehow pulling at her, drawing her in. "What happened after he disappeared?"

“There seems to be an eyewitness account," the director said, his voice betraying a hint of unease. “A young woman claiming she saw him near the clock tower. She says he was… agitated." 
 
Elin took it all in – the urgency in his tone, the photo's ominous presence, and that chilling detail about the dead man being "agitated". Something was changing; something was amiss, and the wind seemed to hold a whispered warning as she looked at the photograph.

"Mikael?" she asked, her voice tinged with uncertainty. 

"He’s in contact," the director replied. He held out his phone.  “But it is important that you speak directly with him.”

The image of Mikael's face swam before her eyes as he continued, "Time is short, Elin. The clock is ticking."

As she glanced at the photo again and again, a wave of fear washed over her. There was something sinister in the director’s gaze; an underlying tension that tightened her chest as though the very air had become thick with impending doom. 
“There's another piece of the puzzle," he whispered, his eyes filled with an unspoken gravity. "Something vital." The room seemed to spin around her. This was not just about a dead man anymore.

Her eyes narrowed; she felt a surge of determination.  "What is it?" she demanded, the words sharp and cold as she clutched the file in her hand.  She needed answers, and the clock tower’s shadow loomed over their impending journey.

“The woman. The one he saw," the director murmured. "He mentioned her a few times." 

A chill ran down her spine. It was like a forgotten memory from long ago. Then it struck her – she knew the man in the photo. He looked strikingly familiar, almost as though he were someone she had seen before. And there was something off about this woman—she felt vaguely aware of her presence but couldn’t place where or when. 

“The clock," she whispered, a sudden realization dawning on her like a flash of lightning. “Is it connected to the girl?”


Her mind raced as she glanced at Mikael's photo once more and then quickly looked back down at the file in her hands. Her gaze fell upon a small inscription on the corner of one of the pages. The date was written there - today – but that date felt different than usual, an almost tangible sense of foreboding settling over her like the weight of an unspoken truth.
