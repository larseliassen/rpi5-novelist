"He was right,"  Mikael rasped, his voice thick with unshed tears as he handed Elin a folded envelope. It smelled of damp wood and sea salt like everything else in the Director’s office – remnants of an unforgiving Norwegian storm that had lashed the coast for days. A small shiver ran down her spine. 

"He was always right," she echoed, her eyes fixed on him. “But he was also wrong."

Mikael swallowed hard and looked away, unable to meet her gaze. He sat back in his chair, as if the worn wood itself held him captive. "They thought they knew. They thought Anders had given up years ago,” he whispered, then sighed.  “But there's something else here. Something deeper.” 

His words hit Elin like a sudden gust of wind.  He’d always been enigmatic and often spoke in cryptic riddles – but this time, there was something sharper, more raw in his gaze. It was as if a layer had peeled away from him, revealing a man wrestling with unseen depths. A truth that was both beautiful and terrifying to her. 

She took the envelope, tracing its edges with calloused fingers.  It contained a single photograph - a grainy image of Ingrid Larsen standing near the docks, her youthful face now etched with lines of worry.  "They thought he disappeared," Mikael said, his voice heavy. "But they were wrong.” 

His confession was a punch to the gut, leaving her reeling. He'd held back for days, holding onto the truth as if it were a precious thing – yet never revealing it outright. His face had been etched with an unspoken anguish throughout their journey – a silent film reel of hurt and betrayal, playing out under her watchful eyes,  until today.

Elin unfolded the image meticulously, then stared at it with growing intensity. Ingrid’s hands held the image above her face, as if she were looking into a reflection - a ghost within a shell. A haunting look that sent chills down her spine. 

"She knew," he whispered, his voice barely audible, "Before we even began this journey. Before I started asking questions, before anything..."

He seemed to be choking on the words – unable to bring himself to utter them, unable to bear the weight of the truth. The room felt heavy and stifling, like an old, forgotten attic filled with dusty memories.

“Mikael Larsen!”  The director's voice boomed from the doorway. “Your colleagues are here."
