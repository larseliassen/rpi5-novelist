## Chapter 21

“The woman, she said…” The director's voice was thin, his eyes flickering across the file on Elin’s desk. “She claims to have seen something,” he continued, “a flicker of light in the fog.”  A knot tightened in Elin’s stomach.

He tapped a finger against the photo. It was another portrait of the woman – Ingrid Larsen had insisted it was the real thing this time, no longer veiled in the vague description from earlier. This one showed her standing close to the water's edge; she looked older than before but still defiant and strong-willed.  “She said something about… a dream, an apparition.”

Elin closed her eyes briefly, picturing the scene.  The fog had been so thick that night, even the sun felt hesitant as it dipped below the horizon. The woman's face was just barely visible in the water’s edge glow. She saw the flicker of light; she didn't see the man. 

Mikael had visited earlier that day. His phone call a fleeting shadow in her mind now, but his words still echoed through her thoughts: "The witness account? I think there was more to it than that. Something about an apparition and a lighthouse..." He hadn’t given details; just enough to make her question everything she thought she knew.

“They were both here,” the director said quietly, voice barely audible. “Both saw it. But only one has spoken of it.” 
He tapped again on his phone, showing her a grainy image of Fjerdesund. An old-fashioned ship sat in the harbour, its bow facing the rising sun. The camera was focused on the stern; the water lapped against the hull as if trying to reach out and touch the picture itself.  “It wasn’t supposed to be there."

 “They say it's a ghost,” Elin murmured, turning back to the photo of the woman standing by the shore. "A phantom crew."

He nodded slowly, eyes clouded with something that looked like sorrow. "But this was no ordinary encounter. Something happened here," he whispered urgently, his voice tinged with uncertainty.  His gaze flickered away and met her own. “The camera... it’s gone.” 


Elin felt a chill run down her spine. It had been an afterthought when she'd left the cabin to meet Mikael; the director hadn't mentioned anything about a missing camera until now, only that it was "missing" and not "stolen." Now he looked almost as if he were talking about a stolen heart—a loss that made him look hollow.

Her mind raced back over the last few days: the woman in white, the eerie feeling of presence around Anders Bjørnstad…  What could be more unsettling than this? The weight of it was crushing. 
A sudden realization dawned on Elin. A red herring. This wasn’t about the camera or the vision; it was about something far bigger. Something that had been buried beneath layers of doubt and fear. An entire story with no plot, a single scene in an endless play of deception.

“The director's note is... strange," she said finally, staring at her own reflection in his glasses. Her voice echoed back at her; hollow and distant, as if from another place. "This was a red herring." 
She stood up abruptly, the sound of her chair scraping against the floor like nails on a chalkboard echoing through the empty office.  "There is something else here,” she declared, a new sense of urgency in her voice. “We've got to look deeper.” She paused for a second and then continued with a chilling conviction, "Something more sinister lies beneath the surface," 


She didn’t wait for his response; she was already on her feet, leaving him standing alone in the quiet office, his gaze fixed upon the photo of Fjerdesund.  The fog had settled again, blanketing the town in an unnatural silence. The only sounds were the rhythmic crashing waves against the shore and a single bird's call echoing from somewhere near the lighthouse.
 
“I’ll be back," she said to no one. “And I won’t leave until everything is clear.”
