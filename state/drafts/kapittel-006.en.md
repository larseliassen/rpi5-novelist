## Chapter 6

**The Lighthouse Keeper's Log:**  Elin carefully lifted the worn, leather-bound journal from her satchel. It lay open on the rough wooden table beside her, its pages filled with cramped, almost frantic scrawl detailing the harsh realities of life at Langesund lighthouse. A century of relentless storms and long nights spent battling against nature had etched lines into the aged wood, and the worn ink seemed to shimmer in the flickering lamplight.  She flipped through the brittle pages, her eyes scanning for any mention of Anders Bjørnstad. "Anything significant?" Ingrid's voice, soft but insistent, cut through Elin’s quiet contemplation. 

"Mostly weather reports," Elin replied, pointing at a series of entries filled with meticulous sketches of wind direction and tides.  "Nothing that mentions this person, or his past." She gently turned the page where a date was circled in black ink - "October 2nd, 1976". The same date marked on an old newspaper clipping she'd found earlier in her desk at the police station. A sudden chill crept through her as if she had touched something cold.

"There's something about this," Ingrid insisted, leaning forward. “The man’s presence here was... significant.” 
Her eyes held a glint of sadness and understanding, as if they already knew the answers to questions they weren't quite ready to ask.  “Did you ever feel it? The weight of these secrets?” 

Elin nodded slowly.  This wasn’t just about piecing together facts; she could sense something deeper at play – a lingering presence that lingered in the silence as if memories were trying to escape. It was like the journal itself held a breath, waiting for an answer that hadn't been written yet. 

"We need answers," Elin stated, her gaze fixed on the faded ink of a diary entry mentioning "a clandestine meeting."  Ingrid’s eyes widened slightly.
“Who could they be?" Ingrid asked softly, as if hesitant to voice the implications. “Why would they meet here?”

The weight of these questions seemed to settle in both their chests, heavy with the unspoken truth. 

Later that night, as they sat by the old stove in the small police station, a sense of urgency filled the air. As Elin poured over pages from an old newspaper archive, Ingrid’s quiet voice broke the silence. “Did you know about Anders before?” she asked.

“Just what I needed to understand,” said Elin with finality as a memory struck her.  She was reminded by this moment's detail of a conversation with an old woman who had run a bookstore in Lofoten years ago, a bookstore that seemed to be filled with echoes of the past. 

"He told me he knew Langesund lighthouse," she said, “His name used to be Anders, but he changed it.” Ingrid’s brow furrowed as if understanding something deeply hidden.  “Why?" 
Elin paused for a moment before continuing. "He was looking for answers."
