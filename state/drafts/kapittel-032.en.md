## Chapter 32

“The woman in the lighthouse,” Director Olsen said, his voice dry and rasping like the wind whipping through the skeletal trees of the old pine forest near Værnes.  He looked out across the harbor, his eyes reflecting the stormy grey sky that mirrored the unease swirling inside Elin. She felt a familiar prickle on her skin, as if she was being watched by unseen eyes.

“That's what Anders saw,” he continued, stepping back from the window. “A woman in white.  She haunts him.” He placed an old photo of Ingrid Larsen on the desk before her. 

"He swore it wasn’t real."  His fingers traced the edges of the photograph, "But he... he keeps talking about her, about visions."

Elin's breath caught in her throat as a silent scream arose within her. Anders had been evasive, his silence thick and impenetrable. Now, looking at this photo - an image she already knew was etched into his mind - it all felt like a grotesque, fragmented puzzle: the missing camera footage, the witness accounts that suddenly seemed to paint a more horrifying reality. 

The director’s voice echoed in her head as he explained how the police investigation had stalled and their inquiries were hampered by Anders' claims of "false memories," a defense so flimsy it grated on the edge of her nerves.  “Mikael, his alibi is crumbling,” she remembered him saying with a quiet urgency as if acknowledging a truth too brutal to speak aloud.

A shiver ran through Elin as though a gust from the icy water just outside Værnes police station had breached her coat and touched the raw nerve beneath. She’d been right about Anders all along; he was no victim of circumstance but a participant in this twisted play, someone who'd been lured into a dangerous game with consequences that stretched far beyond his own understanding. The mystery wasn't just about finding the missing camera or uncovering the woman in white – it was about knowing why. 

Suddenly, the door creaked open and Mikael stood silhouetted against the harsh light streaming from the street lamp outside. "Elin," he said quietly, but the urgency in his voice cut through her thoughts like a knife. “Anders confessed,”  his eyes seemed to hold an unspoken accusation as if the very air around him was charged with danger. 

A rush of adrenaline flooded her veins, the weight of several weeks’ worth of investigations crushing her chest. "What?" she breathed out, unable to meet his gaze. His expression remained stoic but a subtle shift in his posture betrayed a hidden tremor of unease. He gestured towards her desk, where a small wooden box lay abandoned on the cluttered surface. 

"I found it," he whispered, the words heavy with unspoken anxieties. 

Inside the box was a faded, worn photograph of Ingrid Larsen. Her eyes held an unsettling intensity, as if she’d stared back at them from beyond death's embrace. The image stirred something deep inside her – a primal instinct to protect and avenge, but also fear. Something about it resonated with that chilling memory: the woman in white who had haunted Anders for days, a specter he claimed saw visions of their shared past.  A shiver coursed through her as if she’d been jolted awake from sleep after years spent walking on the icy ground of frozen dreams.

“She was here,” Mikael muttered almost inaudibly, his eyes flickering back and forth between the photo and Elin. His voice trailed off into an uncomfortable silence. 


"She knew him," he finally concluded, his voice thick with a strange mixture of sorrow and unease. "But not like that.” He paused, his gaze locked on her with a quiet intensity. 

“What do you know?"  Elin pressed, hoping to pry open the dam within him before it burst. She yearned for answers but felt she was about to step into a swirling abyss of shadows and truth.
