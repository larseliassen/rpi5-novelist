## Chapter 12

The phone rang, its harsh jingle cutting through Elin’s quiet focus like a knife.  “Johansen?” The voice on the other end rasped, weary and edged with an urgency that sent shivers down her spine. “It’s time.”   

She was jolted back to reality by the insistent tone of the call. It came from a number unknown; it couldn't be anything but Mikael Larsen – she could sense his presence even before picking up the phone, his anxiety as tangible as the wind whipping through her hair in the biting winter chill. 

“Mikael.”  Her voice was a low rasp, barely audible over the din of the swirling storm outside. “I’m coming.” The image of Anders Bjørnstad's face filled her head, his eyes distant and unreadable despite their presence at the edge of her periphery, like shadows dancing on the sea in the fading light.

Mikael gave a sigh that seemed to have no volume, but was somehow heavy with years. “It is time,” he repeated, his voice thin like a sail thread catching the wind. “For you.”

His words echoed through her mind as she drove, each passing second feeling like an eternity in the relentless grip of winter's fury. The old black boat lay at the shore of Fjerdesund; its weathered lines whispering tales of forgotten storms and long voyages across icy seas. The place was eerily silent save for the wind’s howl and her own thoughts - swirling, untethered and heavy like driftwood in a churning ocean.

As she approached, she noticed a figure huddled by the wooden pier, small against the vastness of the sea. It was Anders Bjørnstad, his silhouette as dark and ominous as a raven's feather. The chill of the air seemed to cling to him, like a shroud he could not shed even in the face of this cruel, unforgiving weather. His back was turned toward her; his hands tightly clasped together. 

There was something unnerving about his stillness. He looked...empty. As if a void had settled deep within him, leaving only a faint echo of memory. 

"Anders," she breathed his name out into the silence, and felt a wave of uncertainty surge through her like a rogue tide, its weight pulling at her soul as he slowly turned to face her. "I have come for the camera." He looked up at her, his eyes wide and haunted – an echo of the man who had refused to talk about it.

"Why?" she asked, pushing forward in spite of her unease. 


Mikael's voice cut through the silence again, a hollow chime that filled the space between them. "The clock is ticking," he said simply, as if reading the unspoken thoughts dancing on his lips.  His eyes held hers, pleading with an urgency she hadn’t seen before, before he spoke again, "There are things you need to know.”

Then, a voice came from behind him. A man's, tall and imposing, clad in furs against the biting wind; a dark shadow that loomed over the pier like an ancient sea monster rising from its slumber.  He stepped into view, his face hidden by the shadows cast upon him – a stark contrast to Anders’ usual stoic demeanour. 

 "She's waiting," said the man, his voice a guttural whisper, carrying with it the weight of forgotten secrets and hidden desires. 

As if struck by lightning, Elin knew. She saw it in his eyes; a fleeting glimpse into a world far removed from her own. A world shrouded in mystery, where lives were woven like intricate threads, held tight, then let loose.  A world that was about to change everything she thought she knew.


"What do you want?" asked Elin, the urgency of Mikael’s words ringing in her ears.

The man stepped closer and lowered his voice into a husky murmur, "There's something you need to understand. It’s time.”
