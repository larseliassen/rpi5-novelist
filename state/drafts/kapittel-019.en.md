# Chapter 19 

Mikael's voice, usually gravelly and worn down like old driftwood, crackled with something more unsettling tonight.  "The woman... in your dreams?" he asked after the long silence stretched between them. Elin looked into his eyes; their usual deep blue was now clouded, as if a storm had gathered within him. "Her face," he continued, voice barely audible above the wind whipping against the windows of his small cottage, "it's just there."

His words echoed in the stillness of her quiet cabin - an unsettling sensation that she couldn't quite put a finger on.  The image from the dreams, which had been so fleetingly vivid that night, was now stuck in her mind like a thorn lodged beneath her skin. She found herself reaching for the worn box hidden behind a dusty book, drawn by its familiarity as if it held the key to unlocking this enigma of the woman's appearance and Anders' connection to her. 

The box felt weighty, older than she expected, heavier than just the memories that seemed to cling to it. The scent emanating from the box was more familiar now - a metallic tang akin to rust or perhaps old blood. She'd been so focused on the man’s past and the cryptic imagery of the mermaid, she hadn't thought about her own dreams—or what they might truly mean.

"Anders saw things," Mikael said again, his eyes following the subtle shifts in Elin's demeanor. "Visions, like those from a child's story. He felt her presence."  The word "presence" hung in the air; it was both physical and ethereal. 

Suddenly, a memory surfaced like a burst of lightning through years of fog: Anders Bjørnstad’s face when he had held up his hands for the camera, his features contorted with what appeared to be fear or something akin to desperation. His eyes were wide and wild, as if they contained an unseen terror within them. 

The box in her hand suddenly felt heavy, the metallic scent stronger.  She opened it, revealing a faded photo. The woman’s face stared out from the photograph – her image was spectral yet strangely familiar. She had seen this before. A memory flickered to life—the storm that swept through Lofoten-sjøen as she'd been on vacation with Anders when he was younger; she and her friend were trapped in a small cabin, while the entire world outside raged like an angry sea.

"The woman...," Mikael whispered again, his voice barely audible above the crashing waves on the shore. His eyes searched hers, seeking a glimpse of understanding in her face as he realized what it meant. "Was she always there?" He asked. 

In that moment, as if something had shifted, the dreams suddenly became more than just visions; they felt like fragments of a puzzle – pieces slowly coming together.  A wave of chills ran through Elin as the realization struck her: The woman's face was no mere ghost from her own dreams—the image she found in Anders' old camera, in the box that held memories, and now in the photograph. It was all connected to each other somehow.


The room seemed to shrink around them; the wind howled outside, rattling the windowpanes like a frantic heartbeat. The photo felt heavy in her hand, imbued with an unseen weight. 
"Anders...," Elin began, but stopped, unable to form words as a feeling of foreboding settled over her.  The air crackled with tension, and it was only then that she realized the truth – something wasn't right. Something had shifted, something was about to fall apart. As if on cue, a sudden rumble echoed across the distance; the sound of metal scraping against stone. 

Something in the distance - or perhaps someone who lurked within her own subconscious - whispered: "She's here."  And that thought sent shivers down Elin's spine.  The woman was waiting—and she had come for Anders, and maybe, just maybe, for Elin too.
