## Chapter 8:  The Siren's Song 


“It just feels… wrong, that’s all,” Ingrid rasped, her eyes fixed on the swirling patterns of snow in the lantern light. The scent of salt and pine clung to her like an old cloak; a familiar comfort against the chill of the evening air. "Like a song sung by some long-forgotten spirit.” 

Elin studied her, her gaze sharp as a frostbitten blade.  "Spirit or otherwise," she replied, more to herself than to Ingrid's weary face, "what was Anders Bjørnstad doing near the body?"

A sudden gust ripped through the air, sending chills down the back of Elin’s spine and rattling the old wooden beams. The storm had been relentless all day, a furious symphony of wind and waves battering against Fjerdesund's stony cliffs. Even in her experience, there was something… unsettling about this night. 

She took out her notebook, its leather worn smooth from years of use. Its pages held the weight of countless tales, each line echoing with tragedy or hope, but tonight, all she focused on was the entry for the day. 

***  *Dag 8: The body found in the boat off Fjerdesund.* *Anders Bjørnstad is interrogated by police. No memory of the scene. A faint smell of tobacco and pine lingers around him.* ***
   
"He's not saying much," Ingrid muttered, her voice brittle as glass. "Just murmurs of waves, memories slipping through his fingers."  She turned to face the swirling darkness outside the window, tracing invisible patterns on its frosted surface.

Anders had been elusive, even in his silence. While his eyes seemed vacant when he spoke, they held a flicker of something more: an underlying current of agitation and unease.  The man's story had shifted like fog on the North Sea, refusing to settle into coherent form. 

"Did you find anything peculiar about the body?" Elin asked, her voice echoing in the stillness. She traced her finger along the sketch of the scene she’d drawn - a simple diagram with crude lines depicting the location and position of the deceased. 


Ingrid’s face creased with concern before she nodded, "The angle of his body," she said slowly.  "He's... he's positioned as if he knew this man."  Her eyes filled with an unspoken conviction. The weight of those words hit Elin like a physical blow.

She couldn't help but think back to the video footage they had found earlier; the one that showed Bjørnstad, his hands shaking slightly as he clutched the pistol, staring blankly down at the body before it was being taken out from the boat and deposited on shore.  The video seemed frozen in time, capturing a fleeting moment of horror in its static frame.

“He had an alibi,” Elin observed, a knot tightening in her throat, “But… there's something about him.” 

As if struck by a sudden realization, Ingrid spoke again, "Like a forgotten song," she whispered, “The one that lures you into the water, promising rest and solace but ultimately claiming your voice."
  
A sudden gust of wind slammed against the windows, rattling the glass like an agitated heartbeat. Elin turned her attention back to the notebook, tracing the timeline with weary fingers. The pages held a story yet unfinished, a tale shrouded in shadows and secrets. 

The storm howled outside, mirroring the unrest inside. As the night deepened, the whispers of doubt grew louder, echoing the growing sense of unease that clawed at her insides.  A single thought echoed through Elin's mind - something was off-kilter, a note discordant within the symphony of death and despair. The mystery had a voice now – one hidden in the echoes of the storm, a voice whispering promises of danger and intrigue. 
 
"What if," she whispered to herself, her eyes widening at the realization, "The Siren's song isn't just about lost souls?"  

There was something more.
