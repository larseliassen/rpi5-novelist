## Chapter 26

The director's note arrived in the afternoon, a crisp sheet folded neatly on Elin’s desk like a white dove released into a black abyss. It stated simply: “Anders Larsen was seen by the dock at noon.”  He had been reported missing two days ago, leaving his family to grapple with the silence of an empty house and the echoing emptiness of the sea that cradled him.

As if responding to an unseen hand, Elin sat down and picked up a worn, blue photo from her desk - a picture of Anders Larsen taken decades earlier, barely recognizable under years of dust and time. Her gaze drifted towards the window, watching the storm's fury in its raw, unfiltered glory. The wind howled outside, mimicking the churning sea far beyond the city limits.

Mikael called later that afternoon, his voice low and strained as he relayed news from Fjerdesund. “A police car showed up at Anders’ apartment,” he said, his voice crackling with static. “Said they were investigating the fire.”

The director's office was still an oppressive space even after nightfall. The air hung thick, a cold mist clinging to it like ghostly fingers, whispering secrets in a language that seemed too familiar yet entirely alien. 

“They found something,” Mikael said later that evening. "A box." His voice was hesitant, almost apologetic. He described his encounter with the police at Anders' apartment. “He was talking about something – some kind of message he received, or something from a dream?” 

Elin frowned. The case had become tangled with threads both real and unreal. There were times when she felt as if she lived in the fringes of reality - caught between what she knew and what she didn't understand. It was this disquiet that pushed her to delve deeper into the depths of this seemingly ordinary tragedy, a journey through murky waters that threatened to engulf her.

A wave of nausea washed over her as the clock ticked past midnight, each second dragging on like an eternity. She stared out the window, searching for answers in the tumultuous dance of clouds and rain. It was then she felt it – a shift in the air, a subtle change in the shadows cast by the flickering lamppost outside. 

A sudden flash illuminated the room as if someone had flicked on a light switch – the sound like an abrupt inhale from a sleeping beast. “Anders?” she whispered into the silence. The name echoed through her mind, clinging to the emptiness of the room and reminding her of the unanswerable questions that plagued her every waking moment.

As the door swung open, Elin’s breath hitched in her throat. Standing framed by moonlight was a woman. Her face was obscured by the shadows, but her long dark hair seemed to dance like smoke in an unseen breeze. 

“Anders?” she whispered again, this time with something resembling fear creeping into her voice. “Is that you?"
