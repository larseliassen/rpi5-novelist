## Chapter 27:  A Mermaid's Call

Elin ran a weary hand through her dark hair, her thoughts spiraling like the turbulent sea outside the director's office window. The storm had raged all night, mimicking the tempest brewing within her. She looked again at the box from last week; the one with the worn leather strap and the old ship whistle inside. The image of the mermaid playing flute, etched in intricate detail, was burned into her mind.

The director's note landed on her desk like a physical blow – it had been buried away for days, but now its weight pressed down on her like an anchor dragging her down to the murky depths of doubt and obsession. It read: "Witness account confirmed; young woman claims to have seen Anders near the docks. Description similar to the man in the photo." The photo - the one she'd found tucked away in a box, with its faded edges and smudged corners – was that of Ingrid Larsen.  

Her breath hitched as she recalled the night’s interview, her eyes drawn back to Mikael’s photograph from earlier; his face contorted with an unspoken truth that he would never articulate. She felt the weight of the image on her own chest, like a lead balloon suffocating her spirit. The wind howled outside, mimicking the turmoil within. 

She had dismissed Anders' claim as mere coincidence - but now, staring at the photo, she saw the flicker in Mikael’s eyes; a ghost of something more sinister lurking beneath his polite facade. 

The next day, under the grey skies that mirrored her own mood, she returned to Fjerdesund. The biting wind whipped around her like a scornful lover, its icy breath stinging at her exposed skin as she navigated the treacherous shores.  She stood on the same dock where the witness had claimed to see Anders - he hadn't been seen there since their last encounter in Skrogvær.  It was time for another interview. 

The air here clung thick and heavy with the memories of a tragedy long past, and she could almost hear the echo of the waves crashing against the pier as if trying to drown out the secrets buried beneath.  Her mind drifted back to the box. "Why choose a mermaid?" she wondered, tracing its edges with her fingertips like a lost lover's caress. It was then that the memory struck her: Anders had mentioned the mermaid's song. A haunting melody he insisted only existed in his dreams.

“The woman’s voice,” she whispered to herself, piecing together fragmented memories from last night's interview. 
It was like a puzzle piece finally falling into place; the witness saw the deceased by the docks, and Anders had mentioned a song.  Was this just coincidence? Or perhaps something more sinister at play?

Just then, a familiar voice interrupted her thoughts - a sharp rasp, laced with an undercurrent of concern. The old captain, his eyes cloudy with years long past, stood behind her, his weathered face etched with worry. "She must be somewhere near," he said, his voice barely audible above the wind's relentless howl. 

His words cut through the haze of doubt like a sharp blade, prompting her to follow him deeper into the labyrinthine streets of Fjerdesund - an echo of dreams and secrets whispered on the salty breeze. Her pulse quickened with each step; something was changing beneath the surface, stirring the stagnant waters of doubt and fear.   She stopped before a weathered house, its windows dark and lifeless.

 "I need to see Anders," she rasped, her voice barely above a whisper. 

"Anders is gone." The captain’s reply was as fleeting as a ghost in the fading light, leaving an echo of mystery that clung thick in the air, like the scent of old wood and salt spray.
  As Elin looked at the house, memories of the woman's description flashed through her mind. A faint shimmer of white appeared behind her eyelids, as if beckoning her forward. It was a subtle shift, a flicker in the dark; a ghostly image whispering a secret from beyond the veil of life and death.
