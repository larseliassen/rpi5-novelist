## Chapter 7:  The Weight of Silence


A strange tension settled upon Elin as she stared out at the icy expanse of the Lofoten Sea. The late winter sun, already dipping below the horizon, painted the snow-laden waves with hues of violet and blood orange. The wind, a constant companion in Fjerdesund's long winters, whistled through the empty harbour like a ghost lamenting lost ships. 

She’d found Anders Bjørnstad at that last stop - a ferry terminal in Skrogvær – after his initial arrival on board Fårøy.  He looked as weathered and weary as the old wooden planks of the boat that carried him, every inch of his thin frame covered in dust and grime from years spent working the seas. He hadn't spoken much, just a constant, low hum like a creaking ship’s hull, as he walked through the throngs of people going about their evening routines – a silent, almost mournful figure.

“You said you were here for something specific,” Elin murmured, breaking the quiet.  She couldn't help but look at him, at how his eyes seemed hollowed by time and hardship. It was hard to ignore the weight in those old grey-tinged eyes, the kind of depth that spoke of a life spent on the unforgiving edges of existence. 

“Yes,” Anders Bjørnstad whispered back, his voice barely a thread against the wind’s howl. “I just…needed something.” He looked up at her, a moment passing between them filled with unspoken truths and silent confessions. His eyes held the weight of untold secrets, like an ancient fjord holding an unfathomable depth within its belly. 

“What is it that you needed?” Elin asked, more to herself than Anders, as she moved closer towards him, a small flicker of something akin to hope igniting in her heart.  She looked into his worn eyes and saw the sorrow there, etched deep within. “Did you need help?”


He shook his head slowly. The silence between them grew thicker, heavier with each passing second. 

“I think I was waiting for a miracle,” he admitted finally, as if admitting a sin to some hidden church. “Perhaps one of those that never come.”


Elin looked back at him, her thoughts swirling like the waves on the horizon, confused and disarmed.  The weight in his words felt more potent than any physical evidence. 

As Anders turned away, his figure fading into the grey dusk, a lone lighthouse beam flickered to life, casting an ominous shadow across his retreating form. Ingrid Larsen stood by the small wooden dock, her weathered face creased in worry as she looked at him from behind the old wooden gate of the boatyard.  Her eyes were filled with a kind of sorrow that was familiar and strangely foreign, even for someone like Elin who had grown up amongst the harsh realities of Norwegian winters and the unforgiving nature of their land.

 "He’s here," Ingrid said finally. “But he won't talk.”

A chill crept down Elin’s spine, sending a shiver through her as she watched Anders fade away into the darkness. It was clear that something was about to change, but it remained unclear just what the change would be. As she stared out at the sea, an unsettling feeling of foreboding settled over her. The weight of silence seemed to press down upon her, heavy and suffocating.


"Tell me," she murmured, a sharp intake of breath. "I need to know what happened.” 
Ingrid’s lips parted as if hesitant, her eyes locked on the sea's tumultuous horizon.  Then a sigh passed through, like the dying embers of a fire that never quite managed to ignite.

"You don't understand," she whispered, finally. "We are connected to things we cannot speak of.”
