## Chapter 36

The director's office was a cold, sterile space, lit by harsh fluorescent lights that cast long shadows across his desk. A wave of icy air rushed in when he pulled back the heavy door, an unwelcome gust of wind that almost seemed to carry whispers from beyond the room's confines. He sat behind his worn wooden desk, tapping on a metal case that clattered with each deliberate tap.  The weight of it was as tangible as the chill that clung to him like a second skin. 

"Elin," he started, his voice low and gravelly, yet somehow strained. The silence in the room felt almost suffocating, amplifying his every blink as if trying to gauge her reaction. "You found something." He said it like a confession; the weight of the words hanging in the air long after they had left his lips.

She followed his gaze and saw the silver case held in one hand. The worn surface held the familiar glint of polished metal, but an unexpected tremor ran through it as she reached for it. Inside lay not only a photograph or inscription, but something far more disturbing: a handwritten letter addressed to 'Mikro Midas' etched onto the inside of the case's lid.

"Anders Larsen," she said softly, her voice echoing within the cramped space. 

He nodded, a slow, deliberate movement that seemed both familiar and unsettling at the same time.  His eyes didn't meet hers but settled on the letter in her hand, as if seeking answers he couldn’t find there. “It was addressed to him,” he finally admitted, his gaze distant. "But we don’t know what it means."

She knew she should be surprised, shocked even, by the discovery of this new note.  The captain's belongings had yielded few tangible clues, yet somehow the case and its contents seemed to hold a weight that defied logic. 


It was just another piece in a puzzle that felt increasingly difficult to untangle.

"Did you speak with Anders at all?” asked director Olsen, his voice barely above a whisper but firm nonetheless. He reached for the metal case, turning it over in his hands as if hoping to glean some meaning from its cold surface. “His alibi holds no weight.” 


She felt something inside her shift, her stomach tightening with an unfamiliar dread. The director's words were like stones being dropped into a calm pond: creating ripples of uncertainty that spread far beyond the confines of his office.

“He insists he’s innocent,” she murmured, her voice catching as if the weight of it all threatened to crush her. “But there was something else... Something in the way he looked at me."

"That's what we need to figure out," the director's gaze lingered on her face before turning back towards the case. "Time is running out, Elin.”

A sense of urgency filled the room as if a clock ticked loudly and counted down to an unknown future. He turned the case over again, the metal echoing against his desk. 

"Anders claims there was an attack," he continued, his voice barely audible but heavy with weight. "Something like that." His eyes narrowed, as if piecing together some elusive puzzle. “But I don't believe him."


The director’s face shifted, no longer the familiar stoicism of a seasoned law enforcement officer; now it was painted with a deep-seated concern. He looked at her like she knew something he didn’t, and his gaze burned through her as if hoping to pierce through to some hidden truth.

"We need to find him," he finally said. "And we need to do it fast." 



A knock from the hallway was followed by silence before a new voice echoed down the hall. The director turned, eyes shadowed by the weight of something unspoken.  “Mikael,” he called out. “Come in.”

The door creaked open and Mikael entered, his silhouette outlined against the harsh light streaming through the doorway. His expression was grim, etched with worry that seemed almost palpable as he approached. The photo album lay on the table next to him. "I found something," he said quietly, his eyes filled with dread and a mix of hope and despair. 

“What?” she asked, her voice echoing around the room.
