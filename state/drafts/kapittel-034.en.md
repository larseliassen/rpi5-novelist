## Chapter 34

The director's door, usually silent as a tomb, creaked open like a startled owl's wing. The air, thick with salt and an unspoken tension, seemed to crackle as Elin stepped in, her shoes crunching lightly on the worn carpet. Director Olsen sat at his desk, the shadows deepening into the edges of his silhouette. His eyes met hers, devoid of their usual playful sparkle, replaced by a chilling intensity that sent shivers down Elin's spine.

"Ms Johansen," he began, his voice smooth yet uncomfortably clipped, "Mikael has been… helpful."

His words hung in the air like ice crystals on bare skin. He pushed forward a folder marked with faded, red wax, and she noticed a glint of something – an unsettling flash of silver light - reflected within its pages. A small, metal box had somehow caught her eye before, a forgotten relic from a past she hadn't known.

"I believe he found the camera,”  he said, his gaze unwavering as if examining not just the footage but also her very reaction. "He claims it was locked inside his apartment's safe." 

Elin’s heart lurched; there was something in how he said it, an urgency that wasn't solely about finding a missing camera. The look of the man changed when she looked at him. It made a cold echo of Anders’ own uneasy eyes flicker across her mind and then disappear.  He was almost as quiet as before - his eyes didn't linger for long on hers.

"It appears," he went on, "he also has some information about Anders' connection to the case." His voice, always precise yet with a subtle undercurrent of urgency, seemed more forceful than usual. 

“His father,” he continued, “was known as a… collector. Of things.” He paused for a beat, letting the words hang in the room like a forgotten promise. "There are some reports from earlier years, and..." his voice trailed off, leaving the unfinished sentence to linger unspoken in the air. 

Elin’s eyes darted towards the door, then back to Olsen's face as she tried to process this sudden revelation.  His words were carefully worded, even cryptic, but she felt a tightening in her chest - something that mirrored an unsettling premonition. The director’s office, usually bustling with whispers and fleeting glances between police officers, had grown silent, almost suffocatingly so.

“I think,” Director Olsen finally said, "it's time we take this case to the next level.” He tapped on a file, its image of an old lighthouse, stark against his otherwise dark office décor, flickered into life with an unseen current.  He then gestured towards her, and with every word that slipped from his lips, it felt as if the room was shrinking, leaving them standing alone in the suffocating silence. “We’ll find answers,” he said, his eyes holding hers for a moment longer than usual before turning away. "But first… let's see what we have.”

A car had arrived on the docks just minutes ago – a sleek model of black sedan that sat, as if waiting for someone in an almost tangible form.  The windows were tinted, and the driver remained hidden within its interior until he stepped out onto the cobblestone road and began walking towards the office building with a purpose, his movements deliberate and purposeful.

And then it hit her – there was something familiar about the scene playing before her eyes. A flicker of recognition, an echo of forgotten memories. 


What was this? How did this happen so quickly, without warning? She stared at the door as if trying to pierce through its seemingly impenetrable surface. The air grew thick with a strange weight, leaving an unbearable feeling in the back of her throat, prompting her to swallow hard.

"Mikael?" she asked, his name rolling off her tongue like a forgotten prayer that wouldn't be answered. 


Something was about to change the direction this story would take; something was about to shift the power dynamic and shatter the fragile façade she’d carefully constructed.
