# Chapter 33

The director’s office felt thick, heavy. The silence was more oppressive than the salty air that seemed to cling to the walls of the small room. He'd called Elin in a moment where even his carefully measured words had the weight of thunderclouds pressing down on her shoulders. "There's something here," he had said, his gaze steady but somehow weary, as if facing a storm on the edge of being born. 

"Something you haven’t accounted for," was all he would admit before abruptly hanging up. A cold dread settled in Elin's gut and took root. Was it her? Did something about her investigation make this revelation necessary? Or had the director found a new avenue of inquiry, one that was as treacherous and dangerous as the fjords themselves? 

He’d waited for hours, but the wait felt like an eternity as she finally stood before his desk. He held up two photographs. Anders' face stared back at her with eyes wide and unsettlingly blank; it was the same photo Mikael had shown her earlier this day, a picture taken when they were standing on the docks. 

"The man in the photograph," he said, his voice hoarse, "he wasn't alone." A faint tremor ran through his usually calm demeanor. "He met with someone else, someone who was not at the dock that day."  His gaze lingered on her face as if expecting a reaction that she couldn't quite grasp.

"Who?" Elin asked, her voice echoing in the cavernous office. 

The director paused for a beat before he finally responded, his tone clipped but firm: "He said it was Ingrid Larsen. But," His eyes narrowed with an intensity that sent shivers down her spine, "it was someone else’s name that was on the other side of the meeting."  

A wave of confusion swept over Elin, leaving her feeling like she'd stepped onto unstable ice and would soon fall into a dizzying abyss. “Mikael mentioned something about Ingrid Larsen,” she said quietly, trying to piece together this new information that felt jarringly out of place in this otherwise familiar story. “And the witness described... what?”

"The witness wasn’t just describing an image," he countered, his gaze now penetrating and unsettling. "It was a name." His voice dropped an octave, leaving behind a suffocating silence, "He said it's... 'Kåre'.” He paused again before adding, “But you should know the man is dead.”

"Dead?" Elin breathed, her heart skipping a beat like a fluttering bird trapped in a cage. She had always been certain about Anders’ story - that something was amiss, but not quite knowing what it could be. Now, with this new piece of information, the puzzle pieces started to fit into a horrifyingly clear picture. 

A shiver coursed through her as if she'd taken an icy breath in the middle of summer. The weight of Anders' case suddenly felt impossibly heavy. "Kåre…,"  Elin muttered, finding herself struggling with the seemingly impossible name, as if it was a forgotten melody from a dream that was about to fade away.

“I didn’t realize it at first, but there are two of them,” he said, his voice betraying a hint of worry as he pulled out a file folder labeled "Case: Larsen". 
  “One is Ingrid Larsen,” the director continued, “the other… well, that was... unknown until now.” His eyes seemed distant, lost in a world beyond the physical. He added: "He disappeared years ago."

The director's words hit Elin like a storm wave crashing against her shore. It wasn’t just Anders who had vanished; Kåre, too.  A cold dread snaked its way up her spine as she gazed at the photographs of two faces, both shrouded in an unsettling silence that seemed to speak volumes about their secrets.
