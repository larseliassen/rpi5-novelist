# Chapter 35

Director Olsen had an air about him, something that suggested years spent in the unforgiving glare of reality. It wasn't malice that flickered in his eyes; it was fatigue, exhaustion from battling unseen forces for years on end. He'd been through this before – a case where hope and despair danced a macabre waltz until dawn cracked, revealing nothing but new shadows.

"Anders Larsen," he began, his voice gravelly as if he were grinding a worn-out stone against an uneven surface. "He claimed to see visions." 

Mikael's face mirrored the director’s.  It was filled with a quiet sadness that could have melted even the most hardened heart. 

"Yes, visions," I interjected. The image of Anders Larsen's eyes during his interview still lingered – a haunted look, as if a ghostly waltz had taken hold of him. 

“He’s adamant they are connected to...this,” Director Olsen tapped his worn notebook in his hands. "It seems he saw the woman in white at Fjerdesund," he gestured towards the photograph. His voice was firm but tinged with uncertainty, "and the lighthouse. It's like she exists within his dreams.”

"Did anyone else see her?" I probed, my mind reeling from the revelation of a dream-like presence being linked to the events unfolding. 


“No one else,” Olsen said, his eyes filled with an underlying worry that seemed to have been lurking in there all along. “But Ingrid Larsen…she was with Anders when he saw her," he stated before adding, "She claims she witnessed it too.”

He handed me a worn photograph, the image crisp and clear despite its age. The woman's expression was haunting – a ghost of pain lingering in her gaze. It seemed to hold an ancient sorrow that transcended time itself. 


"The woman," I muttered, tracing her profile with my finger, "it wasn’t just a glimpse, was it? It feels more… significant."

"Significant?" Olsen asked, his voice tinged with disbelief as he looked at me, his eyes narrowed in suspicion. He had begun to doubt everything after the last few months, but this case – it was something different. “It’s been her name on every mention of him, and now... she is a piece of the puzzle.”

There was a sudden clatter from somewhere within the office, followed by silence. 
"What was that?" I asked, startled, my gaze darting to Olsen's face before it slowly began to change into something more… unsettling than just tired. He looked away for a moment before turning his attention back towards me.

“She’s here,” he whispered, almost inaudibly, "But she's not the one I thought she was." His eyes met mine with an intensity that chilled me to my very core. Something in them felt…off. 


I couldn't help but notice a shift within him – an undercurrent of fear that ran deeper than exhaustion. “Who?”

“A witness,” he said, his voice barely audible. The silence fell around us, thick and heavy, as if the air itself were holding its breath in anticipation. There was a cold dread settling over me like a fog rolling into a desolate landscape. I watched him closely, trying to discern what he couldn't even articulate to himself.

"What are you getting at?" 


His eyes darted around the room, his gaze flickering on and off as if struggling against an unseen force. Then, with a sudden clarity, he turned towards me. “It wasn’t just Ingrid,” he whispered urgently. "The woman from Anders' visions... she isn't entirely real."

“What do you mean?” I pressed, my heart pounding in my chest. Something was amiss; the air felt charged with unspoken tension as if an invisible hand had squeezed our very breath.  "She isn’t a part of his life?"


His gaze dropped to the photograph, his eyes widening momentarily before he looked back at me with a sudden clarity. His voice, barely above a whisper, seemed filled with urgency and terror - something I'd never encountered in him before.
