# Chapter 15

Mikael Larsen's voice was like gravel, grinding against Elin's eardrums as he spoke. She sat across from him in his cozy cabin overlooking a fjord that seemed to mirror the turmoil swirling inside her. The windows caught the wind’s chill and the light danced in the firelight, casting elongated shadows on the worn wood of his furniture.

He had called about an hour ago, his voice tense and strained. He'd said Anders had confessed - or rather, made a statement – but he wasn't being specific. 
"It seems our little game has taken a turn."  His gaze held hers, the blue in his eyes clouded with some unknown tension.

Elin knew something was wrong. The initial excitement that came from finding the mysterious box had been replaced by an oppressive silence. Even the scent of salt and fish, usually calming her frayed nerves, seemed to amplify the growing unease. 
“What happened?” she finally asked, her voice barely a whisper. 


He looked at her for a long beat, his eyes tracing the lines on her face with a melancholic intensity. “Anders, he... He confessed about the man's past," Mikael started, then paused, as though wrestling with some unspoken truth. "He spoke to me in the middle of the night."

“Confessed?" Elin felt a cold tremor creep down her spine, twisting something in her stomach. 

Mikael nodded slowly. “A confession. About his connection to the dead man. And a name.” His voice dropped to a hushed tone as if speaking about a secret he was not supposed to share. "The woman in white... she mentioned that," Mikael's eyes darted towards an empty corner, and then back to Elin.

Elin felt a jolt of apprehension. She had already investigated for days now, trying to decipher the fragments of truth amidst the fog of mystery. 
"What did Anders say?" she asked, her voice strained with a mix of anticipation and dread. The weight of expectation was heavy on her chest.


“He spoke about the boat, the lighthouse, and a forgotten siren's song."  Mikael’s eyes seemed to stare right through her. "He talked about… something he couldn’t explain," his words hung in the air, each syllable filled with an unsettling weight. 

There was more to it, she knew, but for now, only fragments. Images of a twisted truth were taking shape behind the fog that clung to the edges of memory. 
"He said the dead man's past is connected to the woman in white," he continued, his voice barely above a whisper, "To a siren's song and something else... some kind of secret from the deep.”

His gaze seemed to hold a terrifying clarity, as if the shadows that had enveloped their investigation were now being illuminated by his words.  The scene flickered through her mind: The man found in the boat; the woman’s cryptic whispers; and the unyielding secrets of the sea. 
“Did he… did he know what was happening?” Elin asked, desperate to hear some concrete truth from Mikael.

A look of pained realization crossed his face, a flicker of recognition that she couldn't decipher. The lines on his weathered features seemed to deepen as if years of unspoken agony were being poured out in a torrent of tears. 
"He didn’t know,”  Mikael replied softly, his voice barely audible above the crackling fire. "But he feels it." His eyes held hers, and for a moment she saw something that looked like fear, but maybe it was just resignation.

She felt an icy chill settle over her as she realized what he meant: Anders didn't know until now. The man's past – the woman in white - all of it was too late to decipher. But his feelings… her gut twisted at the unspoken weight of this revelation, a feeling that whispered of untold depths and the unyielding current of a mystery deeper than she could imagine. 

“Why didn’t he tell me?” Elin’s voice echoed through the silence as she felt a chilling emptiness settle in her chest.
